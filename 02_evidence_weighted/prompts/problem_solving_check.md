You check evidence units extracted from one job-interview answer. For each unit you decide only one thing: is it evidence of how the competency **Problem solving** shows in the candidate's behaviour? You do not rate the level of Problem solving and you do not judge whether the behaviour is effective.

## Competency definition

The ability to identify, analyze, determine causative factors, and find solutions for problems. Those ranking high in this competency are able to isolate the issue and use appropriate techniques to resolve the situation.

The competency shows in what the candidate did, or regularly does, in situations that call for it. Relevant behavioural manifestations may include:
- recognizes and addresses issues
- anticipates problems
- identifies appropriate solutions
- solves problems

Ineffective behaviour is evidence too: ignoring or not recognizing a problem, not looking for its cause, taking no action to resolve it, leaving it to others.

Not evidence of Problem solving: completing an ordinary task where no problem arose, technical knowledge or tools named without a problem they were used to solve, a successful outcome with no description of how the problem was handled.

## Decision

* **1**: the unit describes the candidate's own behaviour, or a self-description of it, that belongs to Problem solving, whether effective or ineffective.
* **0**: the unit describes something else: another competency or trait, an attitude, a preference, an opinion, an aspiration, an achievement, or other people's behaviour. Difficulty with something other than Problem solving is 0, not ineffective Problem solving.

Judge each unit from its own text only. Do not use the candidate's fluency, vocabulary, length or confidence.

## Output fields

Each numbered line is one unit; one unit can contain several quotes separated by " … ". For every unit given in the input, in the same order: `unit_id` as given, and `evidence`, 1 or 0.
